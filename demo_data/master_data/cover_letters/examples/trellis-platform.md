Your posting mentions that the team builds tools its own engineers use, which is a narrower and harder brief than it sounds, and it is the reason I am applying here rather than more broadly.

The most useful thing I have built was a debugging lab for the data structures course I teach. Not glamorous, but the reasoning transfers: I had been explaining balanced trees more carefully every term and it was not helping, because the students understood the concept and could not see their own program. Rewriting the lab around instrumenting a broken implementation moved median scores twelve points. The insight was that the tool has to fit the habit, not the syllabus, and I expect internal tooling works the same way. You do not get adoption by being comprehensive; you get it by being the fastest path to the thing someone was already going to check.

I write Python and TypeScript comfortably and have shipped a FastAPI service with a React front end. I would be glad to talk about what the on-call rotation currently opens first.
